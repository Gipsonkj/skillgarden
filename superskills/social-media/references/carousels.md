# Carousels: Instagram, LinkedIn documents, TikTok photo mode

A carousel is a swipe sequence where every slide does two jobs: deliver one idea, and make the next swipe feel necessary. Pick the framework before writing any slide.

## 1. Pick the framework

| Content you have | Framework | Slides |
|---|---|---|
| A list of tools, resources or tips | Value stack | 4-14 |
| A personal or client result with a system behind it | Result and receipt | 6-10 |
| Several named techniques on one theme | Named-techniques list | 6-10 |
| A strong opinion on a common practice | Opinion with a fairness turn | 4-8 |
| A product or workflow you can show | Walkthrough | 5-11 |

## 2. The frameworks

**Value stack.** Cover states an exact count and deliverable ("12 prompts for onboarding emails"). Each middle slide is one item or category with 3-6 concrete sub-points, same layout every time. Last slide converts: save, follow, or "comment PROMPTS for the doc". Never pad to a bigger number; a tight 8 beats a padded 14, and the exact count is a promise the reader checks.

**Result and receipt.** Slide 1 states a result as fact with a number ("We cut churn 22% with one email"). Slide 2 names the real problem. Slides 3-4 show the mechanism with named tools and steps. Next slides give the literal detail (the email text, the prompt, the template), which is the save-worthy part. Last slide shows the proof as a real screenshot or chart. The open loop from slide 1 to the receipt is what keeps people swiping.

**Named-techniques list.** Slide 1 is a claim that most people get this wrong (with a real stat if you have one). Slide 2 explains why the common approach fails. Then one **named** technique per slide, ideally as bad vs good. Last slide: one-line thesis plus one CTA. Named techniques get repeated and credited; "keep it short" does not. If two slides say the same thing, merge them.

**Opinion with a fairness turn.** Slide 1: the provocative claim. Middle slides: specific, concrete examples of the problem, raising the stakes. Second-last slide: the fairness turn ("I use X every day. My problem is Y."), which draws a precise line and pre-empts the top hostile comment. Last slide: a firm, signed close. Without the turn it reads as a rant. Do not attack something your own content visibly does.

**Walkthrough.** Slide 1: product plus finished result. Slide 2: the pain it removes. Slide 3: the full step overview ("4 steps, 10 minutes") before any detail, which lowers mid-swipe drop-off. One real, cropped screenshot per step with a short caption (mockups read as vaporware). Last slide: the final output plus one positioning line.

## 3. Rules for all carousels

- **Slide 1 is the thumbnail** and competes alone in the feed. Test: would it stop the scroll with nothing behind it?
- **One template for all interior slides:** same layout, type scale and colours. Let the content change, not the frame.
- One idea per slide; if a slide needs two sentences of setup, split it.
- Text legible at thumbnail size: at least ~28 pt on a 1080 px wide slide.
- Claims, numbers and screenshots are real and yours.
- One CTA on the final slide, not three.
- The caption or post text is its own hook (restate and add context; carry the search keywords), not "see attached".

## 4. Platform notes

| Platform | Specs and notes |
|---|---|
| Instagram | 1080x1350 (4:5); up to 20 slides but completion holds best within the ranges above; slide 1 is also the grid thumbnail; caption up to 2,200 chars with keywords |
| LinkedIn document post | Upload a PDF, 1:1 or 4:5; the "N pages" badge rewards an exact-count cover; B2B tolerates denser slides, still one idea each; the post text above is a second hook |
| TikTok photo mode | Same frameworks, faster pace: aim for the minimum slide count |
| Pinterest multi-page pin | Up to 20 pages; lead with the keyword |

## 5. Measuring

Judge carousels on **saves and completion**, not likes. Where per-slide analytics exist, the drop-off slide tells you what broke. When a carousel underperforms, the fix is usually slide 1 or the framework choice, not the middle slides. Track results per framework and keep the two that fit the audience.

## 6. Production checklist

- [ ] Framework chosen for the content type
- [ ] Slide 1 works as a standalone post
- [ ] Exact count delivered, no filler
- [ ] Same template on every interior slide; readable at thumbnail size
- [ ] Real proof (screenshots, numbers) where claimed
- [ ] One CTA; caption written as its own hook
- [ ] Alt text written for each slide image
