# Ghostwriting for others and employee advocacy

> Distilled from: linkedin-ghostwriting (samber/cc-skills, MIT), linkedin-marketing employee-advocacy principles and governance playbook (sergebulaev/linkedin-skills, MIT), linkedin-skills policy on ghostwriting (alirezarezvani/claude-skills, MIT), linkedin-content-writer source and permission rules (microsoft/cat-agent-skills, MIT).

ToS reminder: the named person **knows about and approves every post** and is the author of record. Posting as someone without their knowledge is misrepresenting identity, which is prohibited. Publishing happens by their hand or through the official API with their own OAuth consent; never with their password or session cookies ([tos-and-safe-automation.md](tos-and-safe-automation.md)).

## Ground rules

- **Consent and review**: the executive reads every line before it ships. The ghostwriter never holds their LinkedIn credentials. Use the native "delegate" or page-admin features, or an official-API scheduler the account holder connects themselves.
- **Their voice, not yours**: build a voice.md per person ([voice-building.md](voice-building.md)). On a team, readers should be able to tell who wrote each post.
- **Their facts only**: every number, client name and story comes from the interview or their documents. If something is marked confidential, or a third party is named without known permission, ask once and wait.

## Phase 1: Interview (8-14 questions at once)

| Area | Ask |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who exactly; their starting situation; their main constraint |
| Goal | What this post should do; the offer or CTA |
| Results | Exact before and after numbers with timeframe; sample size; **what can be claimed publicly** |
| Mechanism | The method in 3 steps at most (action verbs); the detail that makes the difference |
| Insight | The market belief they contradict; the common expensive mistake |
| Credibility | What it cost them (time or money); a specific scene; optional social proof; a resource to offer |

Move to drafting only once you have **1 quantified metric, 1 counter-intuitive insight, 1 mechanism (2-3 steps), and 1 defined CTA**. Missing one leaves the post without tension.

## Phase 2: Hooks

Give 3-5 hooks, **hooks only**, and let them choose. Show about 80% of the subject or result and hold back about 20% (the how). Be radically specific: numbers, dates, contrasts, costs. Useful shapes: paradox ("the tax office took X; I opened champagne"), A to C without B, contrarian, behind-the-scenes, a specific date anchor ("1 January 2025..."). More shapes: [post-writing.md](post-writing.md).

## Phase 3: Body

- Re-hook in line 2, then **ABT**: And (context), But (problem), Therefore (solution).
- New information, a number or a turn every few lines.
- Cut "very", "really", "incredibly". Use active voice ("Zombie test": if "by zombies" fits after the verb, rewrite it).
- Vary sentence length; 2 lines at most per paragraph on a phone.
- Close with a clear, directive CTA or a specific question. Not both.
- Then run the AI-tell scrub, but **leave the approved hook alone**.

**Formatting**: no Unicode bold. One source recommends it for easy copy-paste, but screen readers and search break on it, so plain text wins.

## Executive post approval card

```
FOR: <name>          PILLAR: <pillar>       FORMAT: <text/doc/poll>
CLAIMS TO VERIFY: <each number, name, date with its source>
PERMISSIONS: <customer named? internal metric? NDA?>
---
<post, character count>
---
Approve / edit / reject. Nothing is published until <name> approves.
```

## Employee advocacy programme (teams)

Principles:
1. **Scale authentically**: each person posts in their own voice. A 5-10 minute voice interview at onboarding. Don't normalise everyone to brand-speak (third-party data shows brand-voiced posts underperform personal ones about 3x 🟡).
2. **Review the risk, not the voice**: reviewers check facts and confidentiality, never tone, hooks or opinions.
3. **Remove friction**: about 5 minutes per post. AI drafts, the person edits and posts.
4. **Prove ROI**: team reach, team engagement, pipeline (inbound DMs, meetings, deals where LinkedIn was the first touch). Not follower counts.

Review tiers:

| Tier | Content | Review |
|---|---|---|
| A (70-80%) | Personal lessons and opinions with no customer names, internal numbers, roadmap or competitors; comments; reposts with a take | None |
| B (15-25%) | Names a customer; internal metric; critiques a competitor; unannounced news | Brand reviewer, 24 business hours, silent approval after that |
| C (under 5%) | Regulated topics (health, finance, securities, privacy law), forward-looking statements, disputes, contractual confidentiality | Legal plus an executive, 48 h, explicit go or no-go |

Reviewers must flag: unconfirmed customer names, internal revenue or churn figures, unshipped features, financial guidance, allegations about competitors, colleagues named without consent, legal matters. They must never edit: voice, hooks, first-person stakes, or numbers the author witnessed personally.

Audit about 10% of Tier A posts each month. During a PR incident, pause Tier A for the affected team for 5-7 days.

**Coordination limits**: colleagues engaging naturally with each other's posts is fine. Scheduled reciprocal commenting ("everyone comment in the first 10 minutes") is a pod: refuse it. Spread posting across the week so teammates don't cannibalise each other.

## Pitfalls

- Ghostwriter-voice drift: five executives who all sound the same.
- Promising results ("this will get 50k views").
- Recycling one person's story under another person's name.
- Running a team programme on a 24-hour review queue for everything. People stop posting within weeks.
