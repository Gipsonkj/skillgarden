# Career change and positioning

> Distilled from: career-changer-translator (Paramchoudhary/ResumeSkills, MIT), resume-cover-letter special circumstances (jezweb/claude-skills, MIT), job-application-assistant CV guide on domain transfer (MadsLorentzen/ai-job-search, MIT), pitch and outreach commands of interview-coach (noamseg/interview-coach-skill, MIT), job-search-strategist skill-gap phase (proyecto26/TheJobInterviewGuide, MIT), review-resume advice for career changers (phuryn/pm-skills, MIT).

A career changer competes against people with direct experience. The way through is a clear target, the target field's language for work they really did, proof built in the new field, and one sentence that makes the switch obvious. Never relabel old work as something it wasn't.

## 1. Pick the landing spot

The best first role usually sits where the old field and the new one overlap. Examples of common bridges:

| From | Natural entry points |
|---|---|
| Any client-facing role | Customer success, account management, sales |
| Project or programme coordination | Technical project manager, operations |
| Deep domain expertise (health, finance, logistics, teaching) | Product roles in that domain, domain consultant, solutions engineer |
| Writing roles | Technical writing, content design |
| Process-heavy or regulated work | Compliance, operations, quality |
| Teaching | Learning and development, instructional design, enablement |

Test the target against 10-20 real postings: list the requirements that recur and mark which the candidate already has, has adjacent evidence for, or lacks.

## 2. Map transferable skills

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Leadership | Team leadership, budget oversight, planning, performance management |
| Communication | Presenting, writing, client relations, negotiation, conflict resolution |
| Analytical | Data analysis, research, process improvement, decision-making |
| Operational | Process design, systems roll-out, quality, training, vendor management |

For each target requirement, write: requirement -> what the candidate actually did -> the target field's word for it -> evidence.

## 3. Translate the language, not the facts

Vocabulary changes; facts don't.

| Teaching | Corporate L&D |
|---|---|
| Lesson plans | Training curriculum |
| Classroom management | Group facilitation |
| Parent conferences | Stakeholder communication |
| Assessments | Learning evaluation |

| Military | Corporate |
|---|---|
| Mission | Project or initiative |
| Briefing | Presentation |
| Command of a unit | Team leadership |

Example rewrite (numbers only if the candidate supplies them):
- Original: "Taught 25 students in a 5th-grade classroom."
- Translated: "Designed and delivered curriculum for 25 learners, adapting instruction from assessment data." Add the proficiency result only if they have it.

Translation never upgrades scope: "managed $2M in equipment" only if they were accountable for it; "cross-functional" only if it really crossed functions.

## 4. Resume shape for a switch

- **Summary that bridges:** "[target role] with [N] years of [transferable strength] from [old field]. [One proof]. [Why the old field is an advantage here]." Put the domain-transfer argument first; it's the strongest card.
- **Hybrid format:** a short "relevant experience" or "selected projects" block near the top, then a normal dated work history. Purely functional resumes (no dates) read as hiding something and parse badly; keep the dated chronology.
- **Bridge experience section:** courses, certifications, volunteer or freelance work in the target field, side projects with links.
- **Keywords from the target field** wherever true (see [tailoring-and-ats.md](tailoring-and-ats.md)).
- Two versions may be needed if the candidate is applying to both old and new fields.

## 5. The positioning statement (core pitch)

One statement, reused everywhere (resume summary, intro calls, cover letters, profile), so the candidate sounds like one person.

**Build it:**
1. Raw material: their 3 strongest stories, what people come to them for, what they know that most people in the role don't.
2. Core statement (30-45 seconds): the shift or problem they're about -> what they bring (one proof) -> where they're heading.
3. Hook (10 seconds): one line that creates curiosity, not a list of credentials. "I help retail teams stop running out of their best sellers" beats "Data engineer with 6 years' experience".
4. Variants: interview "tell me about yourself" (60-90 seconds: present, past, future), networking (30-45 seconds), recruiter call (30-60 seconds), profile line (about 300 characters; profile copy itself goes to linkedin-automation).
5. Consistency check: the resume summary, cover letters and pitch all say the same core thing.

## 6. The "why are you changing?" answer

Structure: **discovery** (what experience pulled them toward the field) -> **connection** (why the old background is an asset here) -> **action** (what they've already done: courses, projects, volunteering) -> **vision** (what they'll do for this employer).

Good reasons: interests that grew through real exposure, skills that clearly carry over, growth the old path can't give. Don't lead with burnout, disliking the old job, money or "it seems easier", even if true; they invite doubt.

## 7. Close the gaps

| Gap type | Fastest credible proof |
|---|---|
| Tool or technical skill | A small public project using it, with a write-up |
| Domain knowledge | A course plus an analysis or case study of a real company's problem |
| Track record in the role | Volunteer, freelance or internal stretch project with an outcome |
| Network | Informational chats with 5-10 people in the target role ([job-search-and-outreach.md](job-search-and-outreach.md) section 7) |

Plan 4-12 weeks per gap, prioritised by how often it appears in target postings. Referrals matter more for changers than for anyone; warm paths first.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Target role tested against real postings; requirements marked have / adjacent / lack
- [ ] Every translated line describes work the candidate actually did, at its real scope
- [ ] Summary leads with the transfer argument; dated chronology kept
- [ ] Bridge experience listed with links
- [ ] Positioning statement with hook and context variants, consistent across documents
- [ ] "Why the change" answer scripted in four parts and rehearsed
