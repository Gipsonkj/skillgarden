# Salary research, negotiation and offers

> Distilled from: negotiating-compensation and its artifacts (RefoundAI/lenny-skills, MIT; paraphrased, no newsletter quotes or benchmark figures copied), salary and negotiate commands of interview-coach (noamseg/interview-coach-skill, MIT), salary-negotiation-skill and offer-compare-skill frameworks of offer-toolkit-skill (yanliudesign/offer-toolkit-skill, MIT), career-ops offer-prep mode as described in its router (career-ops-hq/career-ops, MIT).

This is coaching on strategy and wording. It is not legal, tax, immigration or financial advice: name the boundary when a question crosses it (section 9).

## 1. Never make up pay data

You don't have live salary data. The candidate brings research; you help interpret it and build a range. Never state a market rate as fact.

| Source type | Good for | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Ranges in postings (required in a growing number of jurisdictions) | The specific role | Ranges can be wide |
| Range the recruiter shares | The specific role | Most reliable for this process |
| Verified offer databases (e.g. Levels.fyi for tech) | Company and level bands | Coverage skews to large tech |
| Self-reported sites (e.g. Glassdoor), anonymous forums | Direction, sentiment | Often stale or noisy |
| Peers at the company or level | Context on bands and bonuses | Small sample |

Benchmark by **level and location, not years of experience**: the same years can map to very different levels at different companies.

**Build three numbers:** floor (walk-away), target (fair market for the level and location), stretch (with strong positioning or a competing offer). State confidence and what data is missing.

## 2. The comp conversation timeline

| Stage | What happens | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Application form "expected salary" | First anchor risk | Range from target to stretch, or "open, based on total compensation" if allowed |
| Recruiter screen | Highest-stakes moment for anchors | Ask for their band first |
| Mid-process check | "Are we still aligned?" | Confirm the range; adjust up if scope grew |
| Pre-offer | Structure discussion | Ask how base, equity and bonus break down at this level |
| Written offer | Negotiation proper | Sections 4-6 |

### Scripts (paraphrase to fit the person)

- **Asked for expectations early:** "Before we get to numbers, what range is budgeted for this role? Once I understand the scope better, I'm glad to talk about where I'd land in it."
- **They insist:** "Based on my research for this level and location, I'm targeting total compensation in the range of X to Y, depending on the full package."
- **Asked for current pay:** check whether salary-history questions are banned where the candidate lives or where the job is (many US states and cities ban them; rules differ, so look it up). "I'd rather focus on the value of this role; my target range is X to Y." Never state the exact current number if avoidable.
- **"Give me your number and I'll make it happen":** stay with the range or the request for their band.

Don'ts: a single exact number too early; anchoring to current pay when changing level or city; "whatever's fair" or "I'm flexible"; quoting a salary website as the argument (use data to calibrate yourself, use role-specific reasons with the recruiter).

## 3. Interview intel that pays later

Ask during interviews: what's the team's biggest priority; why is this role open; what's the hardest part for someone stepping in; how is the organisation structured. The answers become reasons the company should pay more (scope, urgency, impact).

## 4. Read the offer

Get it in writing with every component: base, bonus (target and real payout history), equity (type, amount, vesting, cliff, refresh policy), signing bonus and clawback, level and title, start date, location and remote terms, benefits, notice period, non-compete, IP and other restrictive clauses.

**Where it sits in the band (large companies):**
- **Low:** the loop may have seen gaps. Ask for interview feedback and fix any mis-levelling before negotiating money.
- **Middle:** the standard template offer that most people get. Negotiation works best once a hiring manager is actively backing the candidate.
- **Top:** there may have been a level discussion; it can be worth asking whether a higher level or above-band package is possible.

At large companies a compensation committee often sets the numbers, so pressure on the recruiter achieves little. New information gives them grounds to reopen it: a competing offer, a retention offer, a scope change.

## 5. Compare offers on the same basis

| Component | Compare as | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Direct, location-adjusted | Over about 15% gap = real lifestyle difference |
| Public equity | Annual vesting value at today's price | Back-loaded vesting (e.g. 5/15/40/40); refresh grants or none; price can fall |
| Startup equity | Value at $0 and at the stated valuation; both must be acceptable | Strike price, preferred price, total shares outstanding, dilution from future rounds, liquidity timeline |
| Signing bonus | Spread over expected tenure (2-3 years) | Clawback length; one-off, not ongoing |
| Annual bonus | Target x typical payout | Ask what payout has actually been |
| Benefits | Only material differences | Retirement match, healthcare cost, leave |
| Non-money | Rank by the candidate's priorities | Manager, growth, scope, remote, stability, brand |

Compare **4-year total compensation** as ranges, plus career factors: level and scope change, how promotions work, how influential the manager is, team visibility, company trajectory. The highest year-one number isn't always the best offer.

**Startup equity checks:** company stage; the cash-floor test (can the candidate live on base alone, treating equity as zero?); an honest probability of a liquidity event, since most startups don't reach one; questions to ask: "What is the valuation based on?", "What are realistic exit scenarios?", "What's the post-termination exercise window?", "Is there acceleration on change of control?"

## 6. Negotiate

1. **Know the ask:** target and walk-away for each component, and priorities (cash vs equity vs title vs start date vs remote).
2. **Win support first:** for senior roles, ask for a follow-up with decision makers (skip-level, VP); bring questions about impact and ideas from the interviews.
3. **Ask live, confirm in writing.** A call allows tone and joint problem-solving; send a short email afterwards recapping what was agreed. If the candidate is far more comfortable in writing, a well-structured email is better than a bad call.
4. **Sequence:** total compensation first ("I'd be ready to sign at around X total"), then component adjustments (more equity, higher base), signing bonus last to close a small remaining gap.
5. **Anchor with reason:** after an offer, counter with a specific ask a little above target (about 5-10%, not 30%), tied to scope, market data for the level, or a competing offer. Before an offer, keep asking for their band instead (section 2).
6. **Collaborative framing:** "Here's what would make this an easy yes" beats threats. Lead with what the company gets.
7. **Tactics:** pause after the ask and let them respond; reframe "this is our best offer" to "is that total, or base specifically?"; if they offer A or B, propose a blend; trade concessions (earlier start for a larger signing bonus); never answer a surprising number on the spot ("Let me think it over and come back tomorrow").
8. **Honesty:** mention other processes truthfully; never invent or inflate a competing offer. The candidate doesn't have to share offer documents.
9. **Deadlines:** ask for more time politely: "I'm very excited and want to make a fully informed decision. Could I have until Friday?"
10. **Close:** get the final package in writing before resigning anywhere.

**Levers beyond base:** signing bonus, equity amount or vesting, refresh commitment, level or title, first-review timing, start date, remote or hybrid terms, relocation, learning budget, extra leave, severance or acceleration terms (senior roles).

## 7. Nerves

Most people feel uncomfortable asking. Name the fear and reality-check it: reasonable, polite negotiation very rarely gets an offer withdrawn. Script and rehearse the first 30 seconds of the call. Treat it as a discovery process: say exactly what you want, ask, listen to the answer, try another lever.

## 8. Accept, decline, resign

- **Accept:** confirm the written offer matches the agreed terms; ask about background checks and start logistics.
- **Decline:** thank them, keep it short and warm, leave the door open.
- **Counter-offer from the current employer:** weigh why the candidate wanted to leave; money rarely fixes those reasons.

## 9. Offer letter and contract walk-through (not legal advice)

Walk the candidate through each clause in plain words, then list questions for an employment lawyer where it matters: non-compete and non-solicit scope and length, IP assignment (does it cover side projects?), clawbacks, probation and notice periods, at-will or termination terms, equity plan documents. Flag, don't advise, on: tax on options (e.g. ISO vs NSO, AMT), early exercise, cross-border pay and tax, visa conditions.

## 10. Checklist

- [ ] No pay figures invented; the candidate's sources named with confidence
- [ ] Floor, target and stretch set by level and location
- [ ] Their band requested before any number was given
- [ ] Offer compared on 4-year total comp plus career factors
- [ ] Ask sequenced (total, components, signing bonus) with a reason for each
- [ ] No fabricated competing offers; scripts paraphrased to the candidate's voice
- [ ] Final terms in writing; legal and tax questions sent to professionals
