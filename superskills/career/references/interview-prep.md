# Interview prep: story bank, behavioural answers and mock interviews

> Distilled from: interview-coach and its storybank, rubric, gap-handling, prep, mock, concerns, debrief and thankyou references (noamseg/interview-coach-skill, MIT), job-application-assistant interview guide (MadsLorentzen/ai-job-search, MIT), bq-skill frameworks of offer-toolkit-skill (yanliudesign/offer-toolkit-skill, MIT), career-ops interview modes as described in its router (career-ops-hq/career-ops, MIT).

Interviews reward depth on a few real experiences, told clearly, and practice out loud. This guide covers behavioural and hiring-manager rounds, the pitch, likely concerns and mocks. Coding, system design, case and take-home rounds are in [technical-interviews.md](technical-interviews.md).

## 1. Build the prep pack

For each interview, produce:

1. **Format:** who, how long, what type (recruiter screen, hiring manager, deep behavioural, panel, bar raiser, presentation, technical mix). If unknown, prep for a behavioural screen and ask the recruiter.
2. **What they optimise for:** top 5-7 competencies from the posting ([tailoring-and-ats.md](tailoring-and-ats.md) section 1) plus verified company values.
3. **Predicted questions (7-10)**, mapped to stories.
4. **Likely concerns (ranked)** with counters.
5. **Questions to ask them (5).**
6. **Day-of cheat sheet:** pitch, 3 key stories, top concern and counter, questions to ask.

### Company claims: three tiers

| Tier | Source | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | Company's own careers or values page, blog, the posting, what the recruiter told the candidate, interviewer profiles the user shared | "Their careers page says..." |
| Widely documented | Well-known public frameworks (for example Amazon's Leadership Principles) | "This is widely documented..." |
| Unknown | Anything else | Say you don't know; suggest asking the recruiter "Which competencies does each round assess?" |

Never invent round counts, formats or culture claims.

## 2. The story bank

Aim for **8-12 stories**, at least 5 of them strong, covering different projects, employers and skills.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID and title | S007 "Killed the feature everyone wanted" (memorable, specific) |
| Primary and secondary skill | Data-driven decision; influence without authority |
| Impact | The outcome, quantified if true |
| Stakes | What could have gone wrong; why it mattered |
| Earned insight | What they learned that most people in the role wouldn't know |
| Strength 1-5 | 5 unique and quantified; 3 solid but generic; 1 retire |
| Use count, last used | Track overuse across a loop |

Standard skill tags: leadership, collaboration, problem-solving, data-driven decisions, communication, technical depth, strategic thinking, ambiguity, conflict, customer focus, innovation, delivery, mentoring, influence without authority.

**Finding stories:** ask reflective prompts one at a time: a time they were at their best; the hardest situation they navigated; something that wouldn't have happened without them; a time they changed their mind; a decision they'd make differently. These surface better material than "list your achievements". The brag document ([career-growth.md](career-growth.md)) is a good source.

**Health check:** at least 2 quantified outcomes, at least 1 real failure story, no single job supplying every story, every key competency covered by a 4+ story, nothing used more than 3 times in the same search.

## 3. Answer structure

**STAR, plus learning:** Situation (2-3 sentences) -> Task (their responsibility, 1-2) -> Action (what *they* did, 3-5, specific) -> Result (outcome with numbers if true) -> what they learned and how they now work differently.

- Headline first: "The short version: I cut onboarding drop-off by a third by killing the wizard. Here's how."
- 1-2 minutes for most answers; have a 30-second and a 3-minute version of the top stories.
- "I" for their actions, "we" only for the team's; interviewers probe the difference.
- Alternatives considered and why they chose this path lift an answer from good to strong.

**Question types:** "Tell me about a time..." (behavioural: use a story); "How would you approach..." (hypothetical: state assumptions, structure, tie to a real example); "Imagine you're..." (situational: same).

### Scoring rubric (1-5 on each)

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance | Generic claim | Specific, no numbers | Quantified, alternatives weighed, rationale, outcome |
| Structure | Rambling | Clear STAR, choppy | Headline, setup, conflict, resolution, impact |
| Relevance | Answers a different question | Right story, 30-40% noise | Every sentence serves the question |
| Credibility | Exaggerated, all "we" | Specific but unproven | Numbers, artefacts, others' validation, real constraints |
| Differentiation | Any prepared candidate could say it | Real details, no insight | Only this person could give this answer |

## 4. When there is no good story

Never invent one. Pick a pattern:
- **Adjacent bridge:** "I haven't faced exactly that; the closest was X, and what I'd carry over is Y."
- **Honest hypothetical:** "I haven't done this. Here's how I'd approach it, based on Z, and what I'd want to learn first."
- **Reframe to strength:** "That isn't my strongest area; here's what I bring for the same underlying need."
- **Growth narrative:** "It's my current development area; here's what I've started doing."

Buying time is fine: "Let me think of the best example for a moment."

## 5. "Tell me about yourself" and the pitch

Present -> past -> future in 60-90 seconds: what they do now and the strongest proof, the relevant thread from the past, why this role is the logical next step. Not a chronological walk through the resume. Keep shorter variants: 10-second hook, 30-45 seconds for networking, about 300 characters for a profile line. Build the core statement in [career-change.md](career-change.md) section 5.

## 6. Concerns, ranked

Generate from the resume (gaps, short tenures, domain switches, level mismatch), story-bank gaps and posting requirements the candidate lacks. Ask the candidate which concerns they expect first, then add the rest.

| Severity | Example | Prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Dealbreaker | Missing a core required skill; very short recent tenure | Direct answer, answer to the indirect probe, answer to the follow-up challenge, best story |
| Significant | No direct industry experience | Same three counters |
| Minor | Older role change | One-line answer |

For a firing, gap or short tenure: brief honest fact -> what they learned -> how it shapes their work now. No blame, no over-explaining. Script the exact words and rehearse them aloud.

Common tough questions: why are you leaving; you don't have X; biggest weakness (a real one plus what they're doing about it); why this company (specific, verified, never generic); where in 5 years (ambition aligned with the role's path).

## 7. Questions to ask them

Pick 5, tailored: what success looks like at 6 and 12 months; the biggest challenge facing the team now; why the role is open; how work moves from idea to production; how the team gives feedback and grows people; the balance of new work vs maintenance; what people who thrive here have in common. These also gather information useful in negotiation.

## 8. Mock interviews

1. Agree format, company, role and difficulty. State coaching limits for technical rounds ("I'll assess how you communicate; a peer in your field should judge technical correctness").
2. Ask **one question at a time**; wait for the answer before the next.
3. No feedback between questions. Escalate difficulty, include one curveball and one question aimed at a known gap or the top concern.
4. Act like an interviewer: follow up on strong threads, redirect vague ones ("What was your specific role?"), challenge contradictions.
5. Before scoring, ask for their self-assessment: overall (strong hire / hire / mixed / no hire), best and weakest answer.
6. Debrief: overall signal, arc (energy, pacing, story variety: flag under 80% unique stories), per-question scores on the rubric, strongest moment, missed opportunity. Strengths first, then gaps, then one priority move.
7. Offer one redo of the weakest answer and show before and after.

**Panel mode:** 2-3 named personas with distinct styles (skeptic, ally, silent observer, time-pressed exec); prefix each question with the persona.

**Rapid retrieval drill:** 10 questions, 10 seconds each to name the story ID and its opening line. Repeat weekly until instant.

## 9. After the interview

- **Same-day debrief:** questions asked, stories used, what landed, what felt weak, interviewer names and callbacks, signals about next steps. Update story use counts.
- **Thank-you notes:** within 24 hours, one specific callback each ([job-search-and-outreach.md](job-search-and-outreach.md) section 9).
- **Calibrate, don't spiral:** the candidate's read right after is usually too harsh on some answers and too generous on others. Turn it into practice items.

## 10. Checklist

- [ ] Format and assessed competencies known, or marked unknown
- [ ] 8-12 stories, 5+ strong, mapped to predicted questions without repeats
- [ ] Every company claim sourced or labelled unknown
- [ ] Top concerns ranked with direct, indirect and follow-up counters
- [ ] Pitch in 10-second, 60-90 second and networking versions
- [ ] Mock run one question at a time, scored on 5 dimensions, self-assessment first
- [ ] Nothing fabricated: no invented stories, numbers or company facts
